Lesson 1: Trading volatility with variance swaps

A variance swap lets you trade realized volatility directly. At expiry, the long side receives the difference between realized variance and a pre-agreed strike, so it is a pure bet on how much the market will move.

The payoff

Payoff = Nvar × (σ²realized − K²var), where volatilities are quoted in percentage points. Traders think in vega notional, the profit for a one-point move in volatility near the strike. The conversion is Nvar = Vega notional ÷ (2K).

InstrumentPayoff in realized vol σShape
Variance swapNvar(σ² − K²)Convex: gains grow faster than losses
Volatility swapVega × (σ − K)Linear in volatility

Because variance is convex in volatility, the long variance swap gains more when volatility spikes than it loses when volatility falls by the same amount. The variance strike therefore trades above the corresponding volatility swap strike, and the difference is the convexity premium.

Lesson 2: Spanning variance with options

A variance swap needs no view on the volatility model. Its fair strike can be replicated with a strip of out-of-the-money options, weighted by 1/K², plus dynamic trading in the underlying.

The replication formula

For a diffusion with zero rates, the fair variance strike is K²var = (2/T) × [∫ Put(K)/K² dK below the forward + ∫ Call(K)/K² dK above it]. The 1/K² weights mean low strikes carry the most weight per option, which is why the skew raises the fair strike above the at-the-money volatility.

In practice only a finite set of strikes trades. Truncating the range or using wide strike spacing leaves part of the integral out and biases the fair strike downward.

Lesson 3: Jumping for better volatility estimation

Realized variance adds up squared returns, so a single jump can dominate it. Jump-robust estimators separate the smooth diffusive part of variance from the discontinuous part.

Realized variance versus bipower variation

  • Realized variance (RV) = Σ ri². It converges to diffusive variance plus the sum of squared jumps.
  • Bipower variation (BV) = (π/2) Σ |ri||ri−1|. A jump appears in only one factor of each product, so its impact shrinks, and BV converges to diffusive variance.
  • Jump component ≈ max(RV − BV, 0). Returns far above a multiple of the local BV-based volatility can be flagged as jumps.

Lesson 4: The order statistics estimator in action

Quantile-based estimators split the day into blocks and look at order statistics of absolute returns within each block. The median and other quantiles ignore extreme values, so jumps barely move them.

How it works

  1. Split n returns into blocks of m consecutive returns.
  2. In each block, take the p-quantile of |r|. For normal returns, it equals σ × zp, where zp is the matching quantile of |N(0,1)|.
  3. Estimate the block volatility as quantile ÷ zp, square it, multiply by m, and sum over blocks.

A jump can contaminate at most one value per block, so unless it moves the chosen order statistic it has no effect. Smaller blocks isolate jumps better; larger blocks give a more efficient estimate. Higher quantiles use more information but are closer to the extremes.
